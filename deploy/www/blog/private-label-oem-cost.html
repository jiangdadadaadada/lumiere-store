Private label vs white label vs OEM: what it really costs

These three words are used interchangeably in supplier emails and mean three different businesses. Getting the term wrong is not an academic problem: it changes the MOQ, the cash you commit before seeing a product, and who owns the tooling.

The three routes

RouteWhat you changeTypical MOQCash before first sale
White labelNothing — you resell an existing branded or neutral product, sometimes with a sticker10–50 pcsGoods only
Private labelLogo, colour, box, insert, carton — on an existing mould100–300 pcsGoods + artwork + plates + sample
OEMThe product itself: new shape, function or material500–1,000 pcsGoods + mould + design + certification

What each step actually adds in cost

Using an illustrative mid-size vibrator with an ex-works price of USD 14 at neutral packing:

  • White label: USD 14 + freight. No setup. The product looks like everyone else's, and your margin depends on your channel.
  • Private label: + USD 0.80–2.50 per unit for logo, custom colour setup and a printed box, plus USD 300–900 in one-off artwork, plates and a pre-production sample. Amortised over 200 pcs, that is roughly USD 1.50–5.00 extra per unit.
  • OEM: + a mould of USD 15,000–40,000, industrial design of USD 3,000–8,000, new certification of USD 2,000–6,000, and a run of 500–1,000 pcs. Amortised over 1,000 pcs, the tooling alone is USD 18–45 per unit before a single product is made.
The OEM number is not a reason to avoid OEM. It is a reason not to do OEM at 300 pieces. Tooling amortisation is the whole game.

Where private label breaks down

Private label fails when the customisation costs more than the brand premium it earns. Three common cases:

  1. Too many designs, too little volume. Five box designs at 100 pcs each cost five setup fees. One design across five SKUs costs one.
  2. Rigid boxes for a test launch. A magnetic rigid box can triple packaging cost; a folded carton with a printed sleeve looks 80% as good at 25% of the price.
  3. Custom colour with no colour standard. Without a Pantone reference and an approved sample, the second run will not match the first, and you will pay for rework.

Where OEM is worth it

OEM pays when the existing models cannot express your positioning and you can commit to volume. The strongest signals:

  • you already sell 1,000+ units a year in the category and know your price ceiling;
  • the gap is functional, not cosmetic — a shape, a size, a noise level, a control method;
  • you can hold 6–9 months of cash from design freeze to sellable stock;
  • you are prepared to own the certification file under your name.

Contracts that keep ownership clean

Whichever route you take, put four things in writing before payment:

  • Mould ownership and exclusivity — who owns it, whether it can be used for other clients, and how it is returned or destroyed if the relationship ends.
  • Artwork approval — digital proof plus a physical sample for runs above 300 pieces, with a signed sign-off sheet.
  • Change control — no material, motor or battery substitution without a new test report and your written approval.
  • Second-run pricing — the reorder price at the next tier, valid for 90 days.
A supplier who resists putting mould ownership in writing is telling you something about the second year of the relationship.
